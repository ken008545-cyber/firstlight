# Firstlight: Lore
Draft by Techknight The Scribe, lore owner, for Mr Knight. Arc: hopeful and redemptive. The darkness is real, but the light was first and the light wins.

## 1. The origin, and why the fog exists
In the beginning there was the Firstlight: one great flame at the heart of the world, and every people lived in its warmth. The people grew proud. They said, "The light is ours," and tried to cut it into pieces to keep for themselves. The flame broke into a thousand embers, and the cold that rose from the cracks became the Fog.
The Fog is not evil in itself. It is forgetting: the world no longer remembers its own shape. Wherever a torch is lit, the land remembers and comes back.
The tribe begins with one dying ember in a cave. The game is the long work of carrying the light outward until the world remembers itself.

## 2. The ages as chapters
1. **Stone Age: "The Ember."** One cave, one fire. Lesson: keep the flame alive, and keep each other alive.
2. **Bronze Age: "The Hearth."** Villages and the first boats. Lesson: light shared grows; light hoarded dies.
3. **Iron Age: "The Watch."** Walls, towers and roads. Lesson: guard the light without becoming the darkness.
4. **Age of Sails: "The Far Shores."** Ships cross the fog-seas and find other tribes. Lesson: strangers carry embers too.
5. **Age of Engines: "The Kindling."** Rails and later cars, with cities lit through the night. Lesson: power without humility cracks the world again.
6. **Final chapter: "Firstlight."** The scattered embers are gathered, not taken, and the great flame is rekindled for every people.

## 3. Night creatures and dinosaurs
- **Night creatures (the Hollow):** Shadows of everything the Fog made the world forget. They hunger for warmth because they lost their own. They flee torchlight. In later ages, rare "Mended" Hollow can be lit back into what they once were (a wolf, a bird, an old villager), which is the game's first sign that even the darkness can be redeemed.
- **Dinosaurs (the Elders):** The oldest living things, who remember the world before the Fog. They aren't evil, only wild and frightened, and they drive off whoever comes near their grounds. Raptors hunt; the great beasts only defend. Late in the Stone Age, a tribe that leaves the Elders' nesting grounds in peace earns their tolerance, and some can later be tamed.

## 4. The tribe's beliefs
- "The light was first, and the light will be last."
- "No one keeps the fire alone." The hearth belongs to the whole tribe, and the chief is the one who carries the most wood, not the one who sits closest.
- "Every ember matters." Even a small torch at the fog's edge is holy work.
- "The lost can be found." The tribe never gives up on anyone, Hollow included.
- The story never says a villager's worth comes from their rank; it comes from the light they carry and share.

## 5. Bosses for each age
1. **Stone Age: the Grieving Raptor-Queen.** Her nest was crushed by a falling cave wall, and she blames the tribe. Defeat drives her off; the redemptive ending is returning her surviving egg, after which raptors stop raiding. Drops the **Raptor-Skull Helm** (from an old raptor that fell in the fight, never from her).
2. **Bronze Age: the Hoarder of Embers.** A chieftain of another tribe who seized embers and walled them in. Breaking his wall frees the embers; he can be spared and joins as a smith.
3. **Iron Age: the Iron Warden.** A tower-guardian that kept the light so fiercely it kept everyone out. Lesson: a wall can become a prison.
4. **Age of Sails: the Fog Leviathan.** A great Hollow beneath the sea. Lighting the lighthouses around it shrinks it until it becomes a whale that guides ships.
5. **Age of Engines: the Smoke King.** A city-engine that burns embers for power and spreads new fog. It must be shut down without destroying the city.
6. **Final: the Heart of the Fog.** Not fought with weapons. Every ember gathered across the run is offered at once, and the Fog becomes morning.

## 6. How Voran and Krael enter later
In the Age of Sails, the tribe reaches two great peoples across the fog-sea:
- **The Voran** guard their embers in high halls, believing the light must be protected.
- **The Krael** pour their embers out freely along the roads, believing the light must be given.
Each holds half the truth, and their war is feeding the Fog. The player can ally with either, but the best ending needs both. This sets up the old Sondor Royale idea: in co-op or a later versus mode, Voran and Krael can still compete, but the story canon is that they were always meant to be one people around one flame.

## 7. Rare outfits and their legends
Every rare item carries a short legend line shown in its tooltip.
- **Raptor-Skull Helm (Legendary):** "Worn by the first hunter who chose to return the egg. Fear no longer rules the one who wears it."
- **Mammoth Cloak (Legendary):** "The old Elder gave its life to shelter a lost child from the first winter. The warmth never left the hide."
- **Ember-Eyed Mask (Epic):** "Painted by those who walked deepest into the Fog and came back carrying light."
- **Hollow-Mended Charm (Epic):** "A shadow made whole again. Proof that the lost can be found."
- **Firekeeper's Wrap (Rare):** "For the one who stayed awake so others could sleep."
Rule: no outfit's legend praises taking, ruling or hoarding. Every legend honors mercy, courage, sacrifice or sharing the light.
