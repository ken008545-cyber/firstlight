# Boonboon's Idea Notebook (paper only, no code)

## Chests to discover
- **Old Prospector's Crate** (near caves): pickaxe head, 3 copper ore, a torn map scrap pointing to an ore vein.
- **Drowned Fisher's Box** (riverbanks, found by digging near water): fishing line, dried fish, boat plank blueprint.
- **Shepherd's Trunk** (meadows): wool, shears, a bell that makes nearby sheep follow you.
- **Lantern Chest** (underground only): candles, glowstone shard, a note: "Light it and they'll find you."
- **Rare: the Firstlight Chest**: a sunrise-gold seed that grows a tree whose fruit restores full food.

## Trader characters
- **Mabel Tinwhistle**: wandering tinker with a mule cart; trades rail pieces and minecart wheels for scrap.
- **Old Gideon**: river ferryman; sells boats, and once a week offers a ride to an island with a chest.
- **Pip & Posy**: twin kids at the village fence; swap eggs and seeds for shiny stones.
- **The Quiet Miner**: only appears underground at night; trades ores for food.

## Village names
Dawnhollow, Brookstead, Lamplight Ridge, Saltmarsh Crossing, Hearthwell, Morrow's Rest, Cinderfield.

## Claiming villages with beds
- Sleeping in a village bed 3 nights claims it; a little banner with your color goes up.
- Claimed villagers start leaving small gifts on your doorstep.

## Animals and travel
- Horses you tame by feeding apples; each one gets a random name (Biscuit, Thunderfoot, Clover).
- Minecart rails that hum when a cart is coming, so you don't get run over.
- Boats that can tow a small raft for carrying a chest.

## Little surprises
- A rare firefly swarm at dusk that leads you to a hidden chest.
- Walk-in houses with a chalk drawing on the wall that changes each time you come home.
- Digging deep enough finds an old abandoned underground base with a diary.
- Rainbow after rain that points toward a trader.

## Village-building: the heart of the game (inspired by A Kingdom for Keflings)
- **Villagers gather, you build and plan.** Villagers chop wood, quarry stone, shear sheep and fish; the player designs buildings, lays roads and decides what comes next.
- **The spark: a treasure chest.** Every village starts where a chest is found. Opening it reveals a hut blueprint and a first villager who stays to settle.
- **Growth stages.** Camp (a few huts) to Hamlet to Village to Town, each stage needing more resources delivered.
- **Each building unlocks something.**
  - Market stall: a trader moves in with rotating stock that changes each week.
  - River dock: boats, plus Old Gideon's ferry to the island chest.
  - Smithy: ores become tools and minecart rails.
  - Stable: tame and keep horses.
  - Inn with beds: sleep 3 nights to fully claim the village.
- **Local land claims.** Small claimable spots like a river crossing (toll bridge), a market stall, a mine entrance or a fishing pier, each giving a small steady bonus.
- **Traders with rotating stock.** Mabel, Gideon, Pip & Posy and the Quiet Miner each refresh what they sell, so it's worth coming back.
- **Multiplayer.** Friends can all help grow the same village; each person's banner color shows on the buildings they built.

## Lore: the story of Firstlight
- **The Long Dusk.** Long ago the land fell into a twilight that lasted generations. Villages scattered, roads vanished, and people hid underground.
- **The Firstlight.** One morning the sun rose clear again. That first dawn is where the game begins, and the land is waking up.
- **Who the villagers are.** Descendants of the old settlers who sheltered in caves and on the island. They come back up shy and few, and they need a builder to lead them.
- **Why chests appear.** Before the Dusk, families buried their most precious things in "hope chests" so whoever came after could start again. Each one holds a blueprint, seeds or a letter, and finding one wakes a nearby family to settle.
- **The river.** The Mothering River, which never froze or dried through the Dusk. Villages along it grow faster, and ferry songs tell its history.
- **The island.** Lantern Isle, where the last keepers kept a fire burning through the whole Dusk. Its chest holds the Firstlight seed.
- **The crew.** The player and friends are Wayfarers who arrived on the first morning, following the light. Banners mark the villages they've helped rebuild.
- **Old ruins and diaries.** Abandoned underground bases tell, piece by piece, what happened during the Dusk.

## Gameplay beyond building
- **Exploration.** A fog-of-war map that fills in as you travel. Landmarks, caves, ruins, map scraps and hidden chests reward wandering.
- **Danger.** Mild and fair rather than gory: wolves at night, cave crawlers underground, river currents that can tip a boat, and storms that damage roofs. Simple weapons and shields from the smithy, plus fences and torches to keep things safe.
- **Day and night.** Days are for gathering and building. Nights are darker and riskier, but rare things show up, like fireflies, the Quiet Miner and night-blooming plants.
- **Seasons.** Spring for planting, summer for big harvests and long days, autumn for festivals and trader caravans, and winter with frozen rivers you can walk across, scarce food and cozy indoor work.
- **Character progression.** Skills grow by doing: Builder, Gatherer, Explorer, Tamer and Trader. Each opens perks, like faster building, better trades or taming wilder horses. Villagers also gain trust and can learn trades.

## Replayability
- **Biomes and village types.** River valley farming villages, mountain mining towns, coastal fishing villages, forest lumber camps and desert oasis trading posts, each with its own resources, dangers and traders.
- **Starting conditions.** A lone Wayfarer with nothing, a family wagon with supplies, a shipwreck on the coast, or waking up underground and finding your way to the surface.
- **Unlockable paths.** Pick a focus for each village, such as a farming haven, a trading hub, a mining stronghold or a sea port, and each one opens its own buildings and endings.
- **Random events.** A traveling fair, a wolf pack, a flood, a meteor that leaves a rare ore, a lost child to find, or a mystery trader with one strange item.
- **New world each time.** Randomly generated maps, chest contents and trader rosters.
- **Reasons to start fresh.** Legacy rewards: finishing a world leaves a hope chest for your next Wayfarer, along with new starting choices and harder challenge modes like "Long Winter" or "No Map."
